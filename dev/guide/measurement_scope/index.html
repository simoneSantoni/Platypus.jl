Measurement scope

Platypus measures statistical atypicality relative to a supplied collection, feature schema, transformation and scoring rule. These choices define the quantity being measured. The collection supplies an endogenous reference; the algorithm does not discover a unique population boundary or reconstruct observations missing from the collection.

Match the algorithm to the numerical target

There is no single algorithm for every meaning of atypicality. Even with the same data, local isolation, global centrality, dependence density and graph connectivity can give different orderings. Agreement between these orderings is not an implementation requirement.

MethodRaw numerical targetInterpretation boundary
fit_typicality(...; method=:knn)Negative exact kth-neighbor distance in each pairNeighborhood crowding at the selected k; not a calibrated density or probability
method=:lofReciprocal tie-inclusive Local Outlier Factor in each pairDensity contrast with neighbors; a small coherent cluster need not be anomalous
method=:kdeLeave-one-out product log density in each pairReflected compact kernels on rank support; Gaussian kernels on unbounded coordinates; depends on bandwidth
method=:depthWeighted global Mahalanobis depth in each pairCovariance-based centrality with exact algebraic rank and strict affine support; not local density
fit_copula(...; family=:gaussian)Fitted log Gaussian-copula density in each pairNormal-score correlation model; singularity requires explicit regularization
family=:betaLog empirical beta-copula density in each pairComplete in-sample mixture; untied margins and equal positive weights required
fit_graph_typicalitySelected degree-proportional stationary mass on a full-feature similarity graphConnectivity under locally scaled affinities; low density need not mean low connectivity

Rank preprocessing reduces the influence of marginal scales. Ties remain tied; finite empirical margins need not be continuously uniform. A rank transform does not itself specify a randomization null, and a bounded KDE on rank coordinates need not be a copula density. Distances, boundary effects and the chosen scoring rule still matter. The Gaussian and empirical beta copula families have explicit dependence models; neither fits every possible joint distribution.

The pairwise methods inspect all two-feature projections. Identical bivariate distributions can accompany different higher-order constraints, leaving no population pair-density contrast. This does not prove that every aggregation of pair scores must be powerless, but pairwise density does not identify the full joint distribution. The graph retains all selected features jointly, but its neighborhood and affinity choices still determine which configurations receive low connectivity. Adding a feature also changes the collection of pairs; related or irrelevant features can change summary rankings. Method and feature sensitivity are numerical questions to examine for the intended target.

The summary contract

For each pair, normalization_scores are mapped to reference-weighted mid-percentiles in pair_scores. Larger values mean more typical. Equal normalization values share the midpoint of their reference mass; if all are equal, all percentiles are 0.5.

Normally normalization_scores equals the method-specific raw_scores. Gaussian KDE on real coordinates (:none or :zscore) is the explicit exception: physical log densities remain in raw_scores, while normalization_scores holds ordinal labels of dimensionless kernel masses. Those labels preserve computed order and equality before a physical-unit log adjustment can erase a small contrast. Equal rounded raw logs can therefore have different percentiles. The labels are not density measurements; their magnitudes have no interpretation across pairs or fits. Rank-space KDE uses raw values for normalization.

Export physical/method-specific values with scoretable(result; raw=true) and ordering values with scoretable(result; normalization=true). The options are mutually exclusive. Metadata records normalization_basis=:raw_score or :scale_free_kernel_order. This is an explicit normalization-contract change, not a claim that the original raw-score mid-CDF contract was always satisfied.

Real-coordinate KDE compares direct and shifted kernel masses on a common basis, preserving original reference-weight ratios outside the exponential. Underflowed mixtures are not assigned to a separate low-score group. The numerical comparison certifies the represented mass expressions, not exact original Gaussian densities; terms can still round or underflow after shifting. All 976 independent mathematical comparator checks passed, including all eight mixed-underflow oracle rows. The final 84-dataset audit passed 14,637 checks with zero violations and zero unexpected comparison errors. These results support implementation correctness in the tested scenarios, not construct validity. See the estimation guide. No blanket tolerance merges nearby scores.

For pairwise results:

  • conventionality is the median of an entity's pair percentiles.
  • novelty is their selected quantile, normally 0.1: smaller means more unusual. It describes the lower tail, not the single minimum or historical first appearance.
  • dispersion is their interquartile range. It measures spread across pairs, not uncertainty about the estimate.
  • atypicality is 1 - conventionality: larger means more atypical under the selected rule. One unusual pair can leave this median-based score unchanged.

The median and lower-tail design is inspired by Uzzi et al.; IQR is an additional package diagnostic. Their randomized co-citation baseline is not implemented here. Percentiles are neither anomaly probabilities nor p-values, and their common scale does not make different methods interchangeable. See the source distinctions.

Graph results have their own typicality, atypicality, degree and stationary mass. They do not fabricate pairwise conventionality or novelty. For a disconnected graph the reported stationary distribution is an explicit degree-based selection; it is not unique. Read the recorded component and isolation conventions when interpreting that result.

Reference membership and labels

ReferenceWeights defines relative reference mass. KDE, depth and the Gaussian copula use supported weights throughout marginal transformation, reference fitting and percentile normalization. Zero-weight rows are query observations without reference mass; positive common rescaling of weights leaves the target unchanged. This is a membership-weight contract, not a survey-design estimator, an inverse-probability correction, or a count of independent replicates. The empirical beta copula requires equal positive weights; kNN, LOF and the graph reject nonuniform weights rather than silently ignoring them.

Entity identity and the unit represented by a row are inputs to the analysis. Splitting one row into several records is not guaranteed to preserve scores, even if their weights sum to the original mass: self-exclusion, neighborhoods and effective-sample-size calculations can change. Resolve entities before fitting and retain the ordered entity IDs and source reference weights in the exported metadata.

Each fit uses one shared reference-weight vector. Labels are descriptive metadata and never automatically define reference groups. A string label does not encode graded multiple memberships, and the API does not infer separate reference systems for different labels. If distinct collections are required, construct and record them explicitly. Their percentiles describe different reference distributions and are not a fixed absolute scale.

Time and outcomes

Construct dated cohorts before transformation and fitting. Appending later positive-weight observations can change historical ranks, neighborhoods and scores even if the earlier entities' features do not change. A chronological prefix limits the available observations, but a fit including the focal entity is not automatically a strictly prior comparison. Leave-one-out KDE omits the focal kernel; it does not refit every transformation and bandwidth without that entity. There is no general fitted-reference/predict API.

fit_outcome performs descriptive least squares on selected pairwise summaries. Its optional interaction uses conventionality times 1 - novelty, so greater tail unusualness enters with the intended direction. The fit supplies neither causal identification nor uncertainty estimates and does not establish a historical reference design. It is not a reproduction of the cited scientific impact or music-success models.

Use the research workflow for explicit feature selection, cohort construction, provenance, and deterministic recovery experiments. Save analysis_metadata(result) with exported tables so these measurement choices remain identifiable.
